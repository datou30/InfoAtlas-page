TL;DR

One forward pass. No per-dataset gradient descent, no critic to fit at test time. InfoAtlas outputs MI directly from samples.

~100× faster, same accuracy. Matches MINE, MINDE, and InfoNCE on standard benchmarks while replacing thousands of optimization steps with a single inference.

One model, many shapes. Variable dimensions and sample sizes through a unified architecture. Generalizes zero-shot to CLIP, motion trajectories, and robotics.

Abstract

Measuring statistical dependency between high-dimensional random variables is a fundamental task in data science and machine learning. Neural mutual information (MI) estimators offer a promising avenue, but they typically require costly iterative optimization for each new dataset, making them impractical for real-time applications. We present InfoAtlas, a foundation model-like architecture that eliminates this bottleneck by directly inferring MI in a single forward pass. Pretrained on large-scale synthetic data with rich dependence patterns, InfoAtlas learns to identify diverse dependence structures and predict MI directly from the dataset. Comprehensive experiments demonstrate that InfoAtlas matches state-of-the-art neural estimators in accuracy while achieving 100× speedup, can flexibly handle varying dimensions and sample sizes through a single unified model, and generalizes effectively to complex, real-world scenarios. By reformulating MI estimation from an optimization problem to an inference task, InfoAtlas establishes a foundation for real-time dependency analysis.

Method

A dual-path attentive hypernetwork, pretrained on a synthetic atlas of dependence structures.

InfoAtlas is a hypernetwork: given a batch of samples drawn from an unknown joint distribution, it outputs the parameters of a near-optimal Donsker–Varadhan critic in one shot, then plugs those parameters into the dual-form MI estimator. No gradient descent at test time, no per-dataset critic to fit.

Dual-path attention

The architecture mirrors the structure of the MI dual representation. A joint path processes paired samples (x, y) with cross-attention to capture co-variation. A marginal path processes x and y independently — effectively shuffling the pairing — to establish what the data would look like under independence. An integration module fuses both paths so the critic can score joint patterns against an internally-built marginal baseline. A small MLP then decodes critic weights from the fused representation.

Variable-shape inputs

Real datasets have many shapes. InfoAtlas handles arbitrary input dimensions by padding to a fixed dimensionality with independent Gaussian noise — a transformation that provably preserves MI. Sample sizes vary too, and the attention blocks accommodate this naturally.

A pretraining atlas of dependences

To learn a general-purpose MI estimator, we pretrain on a meta-distribution spanning rich dependence structures: mixtures of up to 60 Gaussian and Student-t copulas (covering linear, nonlinear, and heavy-tailed dependence), composed with randomly parameterized normalizing flows that warp marginals while preserving MI exactly. A low-rank covariance factorization Σ = WW⊤ + D forces strong cross-dimensional correlations rather than the near-diagonal matrices that vanilla random sampling produces. For dimensions beyond what the model was directly trained on, k-sliced MI — richer than 1-D slicing — provides a controlled extrapolation path.

The shift: previous neural estimators (MINE, InfoNCE, MINDE) optimize a critic on every new dataset. InfoAtlas infers the critic directly — the cost of estimation collapses from minutes to milliseconds.

From Optimization to Inference

Mutual information underpins independence testing, representation learning, information-bottleneck analysis, feature selection, robotic policy design, and more. The reason it has not become a default tool in modern pipelines is operational: classical estimators do not scale to high dimensions, and neural estimators turn every MI query into a small training run.

InfoAtlas amortizes that training run. Pretraining once over a vast synthetic atlas of dependence structures yields a model that, at test time, behaves like a calculator: push samples in, read MI out. The estimate is differentiable, so InfoAtlas drops into end-to-end learning pipelines as a layer rather than a subroutine. The same checkpoint handles 1-D and high-D, small-n and large-n, without retraining.

Results

Across synthetic benchmarks, high-dimensional embeddings, video trajectories, and robotic manipulation.

Setting What we show Compared against
BMI benchmark (Czyz et al., 2023) Comparable accuracy to the best neural estimators; ~300× faster per evaluation (~0.09 s vs. tens of seconds). MINE, MINDE, InfoNCE, KNIFE, KSG, KDE, InfoNet
Independence testing (dim 16 / 64 / 128) Matches or exceeds neural baselines at moderate-to-large sample sizes across three correlation families. MINE, InfoNCE, MINDE, InfoNet
CLIP image–text MI (33k COCO pairs, 512-D) Cleanly separated error bands across noise levels; superior sensitivity to dependency change. 5-sliced MI beats InfoNet’s 1-sliced approximation. MINE, MINDE, InfoNet
Motion trajectory segmentation (PointOdyssey) Identifies points on the same object via trajectory MI; AUC-PR competitive with optimization-based estimators in seconds, not hours. MINE, InfoNet
Robotic key-state discovery (ManiSkill 2) Best success rates on Pick Cube (94.2% seen / 82.0% unseen), Stack Cube (68.2% seen), and Peg Insertion (72.4% seen). Task-specific baselines + InfoNet
